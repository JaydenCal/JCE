Stormwater Engineer

JCE Infrastructure provides stormwater engineering for residential, commercial and subdivision developments of all sizes across regional NSW. Services include stormwater management plans for development applications, complying development and construction certificates, on site detention (OSD) design to the relevant council policy, and pit and pipe drainage design modelled in DRAINS.

MoreLess about Stormwater Engineer

Where council sets water quality targets, treatment measures are designed and modelled in MUSIC. Working as a drainage engineer to each council's DCP and engineering specification, JCE prepares plans that are ready for assessment, with the supporting calculations the certifier requires. Straightforward house stormwater plans are typically completed within 3 to 5 business days of receiving complete architectural plans and survey.

Flood Engineer and Flood Impact Assessment

JCE Infrastructure provides flood engineering for development on flood prone land. A flood impact assessment establishes how a site behaves in the 1% AEP flood and rarer events, confirms the flood planning level and the floor levels council will accept, and demonstrates that the development will not worsen flooding on neighbouring land.

MoreLess about Flood Engineer and Flood Impact Assessment

Flood modelling follows Australian Rainfall and Runoff 2019 (ARR 2019) and the NSW Flood Risk Management Manual, using council flood studies and mapping where they are available. As a flood consultant, JCE supports development applications across regional NSW, including the floodplain towns of Moree, Gunnedah, Grafton and Port Macquarie, and provides flood immunity advice for roads and infrastructure.

Hydraulic Engineer

JCE Infrastructure provides hydraulic engineering for land development and civil infrastructure. Hydraulic design covers stormwater drainage networks, culverts and channels, water supply and sewerage systems, pump stations and rising mains, including surge assessment for pressurised pipelines.

Water and Sewer Design

JCE Infrastructure designs water and sewer infrastructure for subdivisions, commercial developments and council works, to the WSAA Water Supply Code and Sewerage Code and the requirements of the relevant water authority. Services include water reticulation and main extensions, gravity sewer design, pressure sewer systems designed to WSA 07, sewage pump station design and rising mains, including surge analysis and septicity assessment.

Swept Path Analysis

Councils commonly require a swept path analysis, also called a turning path analysis, to demonstrate that the design vehicle can enter, manoeuvre and leave a site without conflict. JCE Infrastructure prepares swept path drawings for driveways, car parks, loading docks and waste collection points, using design vehicles from passenger cars to heavy rigid trucks and B-doubles, in accordance with AS 2890 and Austroads.

MoreLess about Swept Path Analysis

The analysis is combined with driveway design, including grades and vehicle crossings to the council's standard drawings, so that access issues are resolved before the development application is lodged. Swept path analysis is provided for residential, commercial and industrial sites across regional NSW.

Erosion and Sediment Control Plans

Most councils require an erosion and sediment control plan (ESCP) with a development application, and the controls must be installed before earthworks begin. JCE Infrastructure prepares ESCPs for house sites, commercial developments and subdivisions in accordance with Managing Urban Stormwater: Soils and Construction, known as the Blue Book, and the council's DCP. Plans show sediment fencing, stabilised site access, diversion drains, stockpile locations and, where the disturbed area warrants it, sediment basins sized by the Blue Book method.

MoreLess about Erosion and Sediment Control Plans

Larger sites receive a soil and water management plan. ESCPs are prepared across regional NSW, on their own or as part of a stormwater or civil design package.

Pavement Design

JCE Infrastructure provides pavement design for roads, car parks, hardstands and heavy vehicle areas. Flexible and rigid pavements are designed in accordance with the Austroads Guide to Pavement Technology Part 2 (AGPT02), and industrial slabs are designed for the forklift, truck and storage loads they will carry.

MoreLess about Pavement Design

Each design is based on the design traffic and the subgrade strength from the geotechnical investigation, and follows the council's engineering specification where the pavement will become a public asset. Pavement design is provided for subdivisions, commercial and industrial sites, rural roads and haul roads across regional NSW, and is shown layer by layer in the interactive subdivision model.

Site Classification

A site classification to AS 2870 describes how much the soil on a block is expected to move, and it sets the basis for the slab and footing design of a new dwelling, extension or granny flat. JCE Infrastructure arranges the geotechnical investigation, prepares the site classification report and completes the footing design, so that the classification and the slab design come from one engineer.

MoreLess about Site Classification

Reactive clay soils across the New England North West and other parts of regional NSW often call for stiffened raft slabs or deeper footings, and each design responds to the classification and to the cut and fill on the site. Footing design is completed with our structural engineer where required.

Common questions

Which areas do you cover?

JCE works across NSW, including metropolitan, regional and rural projects. Site visits, travel and local requirements are scoped in your fee proposal.

Do I need a civil engineer for my development application?

Councils generally require civil engineering input where a development involves a new driveway or car park, changes to stormwater, earthworks or retaining walls, flood affected land, or new water and sewer connections. If you are unsure, send the site address and plans and we will advise what the council is likely to require.

What is in a stormwater management plan?

A stormwater management plan typically includes a catchment plan, the pit and pipe layout, on-site detention sizing where the council requires it, water quality measures, and erosion and sediment control, together with the supporting calculations.

Do I need on-site detention?

This depends on the council and the site. Many councils require on-site detention where a development increases roof and paved areas, so that the site does not discharge more stormwater downstream than it did before development. We check the policy that applies to your address and size the storage where it is required.

When does council require a flood impact assessment?

Council generally requires a flood impact assessment where a development is on flood prone land or within the flood planning area shown on its mapping. The assessment shows how the site behaves in the 1% AEP flood, confirms that floor levels meet the flood planning level, which is commonly the 1% AEP flood level plus freeboard, and demonstrates that the development will not worsen flooding on other land.

What is a swept path analysis?

A swept path analysis is a drawing that tracks a design vehicle through the site to demonstrate that it can enter, turn and exit without striking kerbs, walls or parked vehicles. Councils and Transport for NSW require one for driveways, car parks, loading areas and waste collection points.

What do you need from me to quote?

The site address, the plans or a sketch, and any correspondence or consent conditions from the council. A survey and a geotechnical report are also useful where available.

Do I need an engineer for a granny flat or an extension?

In most cases, yes. Councils and private certifiers generally require a stormwater plan, and many also require an engineered slab or footing design. A new driveway crossing or a retaining wall carries its own requirements. Send the site address and plans and we will confirm what applies.

Do you work with private certifiers and complying development?

We scope civil documentation for DA, CDC, CC and subdivision works applications. Specialist certification and regulated designs are identified separately, with the relevant practitioners and requirements confirmed before engagement.

Can you respond to a council request for information?

Yes. We review the council's request, prepare the civil and stormwater response and liaise with the council's engineer where required.

How long does a stormwater plan take?

Straightforward house stormwater plans typically take 3 to 5 business days after receipt of complete architectural plans and survey. More complex sites take longer. The programme is confirmed in the fee proposal.

What does it cost?

Fees depend on the site and the council's requirements. A written fee proposal setting out the scope and the fee is issued before any work commences.
