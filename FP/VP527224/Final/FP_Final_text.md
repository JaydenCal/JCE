## 1.  Project understanding

Council wants to know whether, and how, a Marine Industry Precinct could be developed on the greenfield site between the two existing marinas and the Marano's fuel facility on Dickson Inlet. The site is about 3 ha with about 300 m of frontage to Dickson Inlet, and the project is to include a public boat ramp and associated parking.

The study responds to the need to broaden the Douglas Shire economy after the Mossman Mill closure, COVID-19 and Tropical Cyclone Jasper, and to the lack of local maintenance and repair capacity for reef tourism vessels, fishing fleets, cruising yachts and recreational boats, many of which travel to Cairns or further for servicing. The precinct is to complement the Cairns Marine Precinct and the proposed marine Common User Facility, not compete with them.

A key outcome is information Council can use for grant applications, potentially with industry partners. JCE reads the brief as needing three things to hold together: a credible market and economic case; a practical master plan for a constrained tidal site; and an approvals, delivery and cost picture that funding bodies will accept.

Existing work. JCE will build on the Port Douglas Waterfront South Tourism and Marine Master Plan (2019), Council's submission on the draft Far North Queensland Regional Plan (including the Dickson Inlet regional biodiversity corridor mapping and Council's case for a south dock industry precinct), Destination 2045 and the Mossman Transition Plan, so that the study extends earlier work instead of repeating it.

## 3.  Scope of services and methodology

The methodology has seven stages that deliver the four milestone deliverables in the Scope. Each stage lists the tasks and who does them.

#### Stage 1  Inception, background review and Activity Plan and Program

- Inception meeting with Council at Mossman, combined with site visit 1: objectives, stakeholders, adjacent projects, communication protocols and the information Council will provide (JCE, with the economics partner).
- Inspection of the site, Dickson Inlet, the public boat ramp, the marinas and the fuel facility, and initial meetings with key operators where Council arranges them (JCE; ecology walkover by the ecology partner).
- Review of previous studies, Council reports, the Douglas Shire Planning Scheme, the FNQ Regional Plan, State Planning Policy, Destination 2045 and the Mossman Transition Plan; GIS base mapping of tenure, overlays, ground elevation, storm tide and flood data (JCE).
- Drone photogrammetry of the site, the Dickson Inlet frontage and the boat ramp during site visit 1 (orthophoto, 3D model and high-resolution aerial stills and video), used as the existing-condition record and current base for the constraints mapping and concept plans, and for engagement and Council presentation imagery, within the airspace limits in Section 7 (JCE).
- Activity Plan and Program within one month of award: comparable marine precincts to be researched, industry partners and community representatives to be engaged, the engagement plan and the detailed program. HP1: Council accepts the Activity Plan.

#### Stage 2  Marine industry research, market and economic analysis

- The current state of the marine industry locally, across Queensland and nationally: drivers of viable marine precincts, industry constraints and existing service provision, including Cairns, Townsville, Mackay and the Whitsundays (economics partner).
- Market review and gap analysis: the vessel fleet and haul-out demand Port Douglas could serve, the vessel classes the Cairns facilities do not, and the facility size this supports (economics partner, with JCE infrastructure benchmarking).
- Economic benefits: types and numbers of jobs, types and volume of businesses that may establish, secondary businesses and tourism markets supported, and the economic drivers unique to Port Douglas and Far North Queensland (economics partner).

#### Stage 3  Stakeholder engagement

- Lead the Council-facilitated engagement program: interviews and industry sounding with tourism operators, marine businesses, the marinas and yacht club, elected members and officers of all levels of government, and community representatives (economics and engagement partner, with JCE).
- One industry workshop in Port Douglas, and engagement with adjacent projects by Council and others.
- A consultation record of key observations, stated requirements, opportunities and gaps.

#### Stage 4  Concept plans and master plan

- Site constraints: storm tide and coastal hazard, flooding, acid sulfate soils, marine plants and vegetation, tenure and zoning, services and access (JCE, marine, geotechnical, ecology and planning partners).
- A landside and waterside functional brief: travel lift and slipway capacity, hardstand by vessel class, workshops, wash-down and waste, storage, berths, boat ramp and parking.
- Three concept plans, compared against agreed criteria at an online workshop with Council. HP2: Council confirms the preferred concept.
- A master plan for the preferred concept covering the base infrastructure layout, location viability, key features and structural works, marine engineering requirements (water depths, groynes, dredging and piling), enabling infrastructure (services and road layout), construction staging options with costs and timing, connectivity to adjacent precincts, and amenity, streetscape and urban design guidance. Marine content signed by the marine partner's RPEQ; civil content signed by the RPEQ (Civil).

#### Stage 5  Planning and approvals, delivery strategy, risk and cost analysis

- Planning, environmental and regulatory approvals from local, State and Commonwealth government, with a proposed approvals pathway and estimated timeframes (planning and ecology partners, coordinated by JCE).
- Delivery strategy options (for example Council-led, joint venture, lease to an operator, or staged public and private delivery) with recommendations (JCE, with the economics partner).
- A risk register for the preferred concept with likelihood and consequence ratings and mitigations (JCE).
- Cost analysis for planning, budgeting and grant applications, based on recent similar projects, prepared by an independent quantity surveyor from JCE and marine quantities.

#### Stage 6  Draft and Final Scoping Reports

- Draft Scoping Report covering the nine minimum content items in the Scope, plus a short grant-ready project summary. HP3: issue of the Draft Report for Council review.
- Final Scoping Report addressing one round of consolidated Council and stakeholder comments, with figures, plans and data for Council's ongoing use. HP4: issue of the Final Report.

#### Stage 7  Presentation to Council

- Presentation of the research, master plan and next steps to a Council meeting in May 2027, in person (Jayden Callen).

### Value-Add proposals

- A grant-ready project summary and next-steps roadmap as a stand-alone document Council can attach to funding applications.
- Cost analysis and staging set out so that early stages (for example the boat ramp, parking and enabling works) can be funded separately.
- The master plan supplied in GIS and CAD formats for Council's ongoing use.
