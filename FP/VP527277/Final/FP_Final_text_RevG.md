## 1.0 Project understanding

Council requires a comprehensive review, inspection, condition assessment and comprehensive valuation of all transport and bridge infrastructure across the Murray River LGA, followed by a four-year rolling program of zone inspections and annual valuations. Every asset is inspected and recorded, rated on Council's 1 (Excellent) to 5 (Very Poor) scale, given a remaining useful life, and valued at fair value under AASB 13, AASB 116 and the Local Government Code of Accounting Practice and Financial Reporting. Assets below Council's capitalisation thresholds (Appendix 3) are still recorded, with condition and remaining life, and flagged "not to be valued".

Council's Appendix 1 sets out the network (quantities to be verified by inspection):

| Asset class | Quantity (Appendix 1) | Inspection and rating | Components (Appendix 2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bridges and major culverts | 62 concrete, 39 composite, 1 timber, 11 major culverts (113) | Level 2 inspection | Substructure, superstructure, deck, abutments, miscellaneous |
| Footbridges | 9 steel, 5 timber (14) | Level 2 inspection, handrails | As for bridges |
| Sealed roads | 1,110 km (urban 133, regional 416, local 561) | Images every 10 m; urban block to block, rural 2 km segments | Surface, pavement, formation |

The network spans four zones: Barham (809 km of road), Mathoura (864 km), Moama (474 km) and Moulamein (842 km). Barham Road, Wakool Road and Moulamein Road are collected whole in one zone, and the inspection extends 500 m past the LGA and zone boundaries.

### Known complexities

- Scale and distance. About 3,000 km of road, 1,890 km of it unsealed, across a large rural LGA. The imaging partner captures at traffic speed so that the field program fits between February and April 2027, ahead of the 30 June valuation.
- Unsealed roads and weather. Wet weather can close or damage unsealed roads and degrade image quality. The program holds two weeks of contingency and a second imaging vehicle if capture falls behind by mid-March.
- Matching Council's register. Ratings, segments and images must link to Council's existing Asset IDs and MapInfo layers. The data review and the Moama pilot settle segment rules, defect codes and naming before full capture.
- Bridges. Council requires inspectors with at least five years of bridge condition assessment on concrete, steel and timber bridges, inspection within 3 m of every component, and an access methodology for each structure. Level 3/4 recommendations must reach Council early enough to act on.
- Valuation timing. A valuation as at 30/06/2027 that passes audit needs condition data and unit rates settled by May. The valuer reviews the methodology in January and the unit rates in March.
- Explaining change. Council asks for every change to its recorded remaining lives or data to be explained. JCE records the reason for each change in the register.

### Stage 1: Mobilisation, Council data review and hold points (JCE)

- Hold point submissions for Council's written release: the written methodology (including plant and outsourced specialists), program with output milestones, list of providers and equipment, methodology for specialist activities, and SWMS and site-specific safety plans.
- Inception meeting and Council WHS induction at Moama, with JCE's agenda and minutes.
- Review of Council's asset register, MapInfo layers, current valuation, as-built dates and WAE plans; set-up of segments (urban block to block with the main road owning the intersection, rural 2 km from zero chainage); mapping of Council's defect schema and Maintenance Management System codes into the rating guide.

### Stage 2: Pilot and calibration (JCE, with partners and Council)

- Three-day pilot in Moama covering sealed, unsealed and urban samples, a footpath run, bridges and culverts, so that ratings, image quality, naming, GPS offsets and the data template are confirmed with Council before full capture.
- Calibration of every rater against JCE's rating guide and Council's 1 to 5 scale, with photographic examples of each rating for each component, then Council approval to proceed.

### Stage 3: Network inspections (partners and JCE)

- Roads, kerb and roadside (road imaging partner): vehicle-mounted geo-referenced high-resolution stills every 10 m, GPS embedded natively in each image (DRFA compliant), wide enough to capture kerb to kerb or table drain to table drain and roadside hazards on both sides, with offset GPS pick-up of on-road and roadside features. The partner rates the sealed and unsealed roads and will state in its methodology whether it captures one lane or both lanes in one pass, including on single-lane unsealed roads.
- Kerb and gutter, footpaths, carparks, roundabouts, islands, medians, bus shelters and rural culverts (JCE): inventory verification, dimensions, condition and defects rated from the partner's imagery and JCE's drone imagery, with at least two images per defect (segment overview and close-up).
- Guardrail and handrail (JCE): inventory, condition and safety data from the partner's imagery and drone capture, with unprotected edge drops recorded as defects.
- Bridges, major culverts and footbridges: Level 2 visual inspection within 3 m of every component in good natural light (no underwater inspection), component ratings, defects, photographs and an access methodology for each structure.
- Drone capture (JCE): two campaigns, 16 field days in all. Campaign 1 (15/02 to 25/02/2027) runs with the bridge partner and captures bridges, approaches and footbridges where the partner needs overview or hard-to-reach imagery (about 40 structures). Campaign 2 (08/03 to 18/03/2027) captures about 200 rural culverts not visible in the vehicle imagery, stormwater outlets, kerb and footpath hotspots, carparks and assets with poor vehicle access.
- Condition 5 and safety defects notified to Council the same day; weekly progress data.

### Drone (RPAS) capture and compliance

- Equipment. JCE will deploy a DJI Mavic 3 Enterprise with RTK module (under 2 kg), with a mapping-grade camera for orthophotos, 3D models and geotagged inspection imagery. Workflow: automated flight paths planned in DroneDeploy (or DJI Pilot 2 or DJI FlightHub); RTK corrections from AUSCORS and CORSnet-NSW, or a local base station; ground control and independent check points at each mapped site to verify accuracy; processing in Autodesk ReCap Pro for point clouds and surfaces, with orthophotos from ReCap Pro or DJI Terra; Autodesk InfraWorks and Civil 3D for design integration; QGIS for GIS and asset register delivery.
- Regulation. JCE operates under CASA's sub-2 kg excluded category, with operator accreditation and drone registration in place before services start. Every flight is within visual line of sight, below 120 m above ground level, in daylight, at least 30 m from people and never over people.
- Airspace. JCE's desk check found no controlled aerodrome within 5.5 km of the LGA; the nearby aerodromes (including Echuca, Swan Hill, Kerang and Deniliquin) are non-controlled. Near a non-controlled aerodrome JCE keeps clear of approach and departure paths and gives way to manned aircraft. JCE confirms airspace with a CASA-verified drone safety app before each flight. - Site controls. A site risk assessment, pre-flight checklist and flight log for every flight; a casual visual observer on road sites; take-off and landing off the carriageway; traffic and public managed by stopping flights when people or vehicles come within 30 m. No traffic control is used.
- Existing condition record. At bridges and approaches captured in campaign 1, JCE will produce RTK-referenced orthophotos and 3D models within the same flight limits, giving Council a baseline of existing condition that the rolling inspections in 2028 to 2031 are compared against.
- Capability. Jayden has delivered drone photogrammetry on previous projects, planning automated flight paths in DroneDeploy, capturing RTK-positioned photogrammetry and high-resolution aerial stills and video, and processing in Autodesk ReCap Pro to build point clouds and terrain surfaces for use in Autodesk InfraWorks and civil design models.

### Stage 4: Data, remaining life, defects, safety and programs (JCE)

- Audit of the partner road data against Council's template: Asset IDs, segments, components, completeness, image naming and GPS, with a re-rated sample in each zone. JCE builds one Excel register and one set of GIS layers in QGIS from the partner data, JCE's ratings and the drone imagery, exported to Council's formats.
- Remaining useful life by component and segment from material, age, observed condition, environmental exposure and historical performance, with the reason recorded for every change to Council's data.
- Detailed defect report with repair requirements and program timeframes; guardrail and handrail safety assessment against Austroads Guide to Road Design Part 6 and current Australian Standards, prioritised by risk and severity; review of the bridge reports and a register of recommended Level 3/4 inspections.
- 10-year capital renewal and maintenance programs by asset category, with assumed budget, prioritised works and risk ranking.

### Stage 5: Comprehensive valuation as at 30/06/2027 (valuer, with JCE)

- Valuation methodology: fair value basis, componentisation (Appendix 2), capitalisation thresholds (Appendix 3) and the fair value hierarchy, reviewed by the valuer before field work ends.
- Current replacement cost unit rates built from Council's contract rates and JCE's estimate data, checked against the valuer's benchmarks; useful lives, residual values and condition-based remaining lives.
- Valuation of every asset above threshold, reconciled by JCE to the register, with working papers; draft and final valuation report signed by the valuer.

### Stage 6: Project management, quality and close-out (JCE)

### Rolling inspection and valuation, 2028 to 2031

- Each year: physical inspection of the nominated zone (Barham 2028, Mathoura 2029, Moama 2030, Moulamein 2031) and of the newly acquired assets Council lists, capture from February to April, an update of the register, defects and programs, and a valuation by 30 June: desktop revaluations of the whole class in 2028 to 2030 and a comprehensive valuation of the whole class in 2031. The same milestones and payment split apply each year.

### Quality assurance and quality control

- Rating guide and data template approved by Council after the pilot, with photographic examples of each rating.
- Calibration of every rater before capture, and again if a rater changes.
- Automated completeness checks in QGIS by JCE on every partner and JCE data delivery (Asset IDs, segment continuity, mandatory fields, image naming and GPS).
- JCE re-rating of a random sample of at least 2% of partner road segments in each zone, and a blind re-rate by Jayden of 2% of the technician's ratings; a rater whose sample differs by more than one rating point on more than 10% of items re-rates that batch.
- Bridge reports checked by JCE against the Level 2 scope and Council's rating scale before issue.
- Director review of every deliverable before it reaches Council, recorded on a checklist; one register of Council comments and how each was closed.

### Data management, reporting and integration

- Structure: Council's Excel data template (tabs Roads, Kerb, Footpath, Bridges, Roundabouts, Culverts, Guardrail, Bus Shelter, Carparks), one row per asset or segment, linked to Council Asset IDs; new assets flagged for Council to assign IDs.
- Spatial: GIS layers of segments and point assets built by JCE in QGIS and exported to MapInfo .tab (or Council's preferred format) with Asset ID and inspection metadata; drone orthophotos where captured; coordinates in GDA94 and GDA2020 (MGA zones 54 and 55), heights to AHD.
- Images: .jpg with native GPS, named Asset ID, component or chainage, and lane direction or facing, with an image index file.
- Drone imagery: JCE's high-resolution aerial stills and short video of the bridges, culverts and hotspots it flies, named by Asset ID and listed in the image index, as existing-condition and defect records Council can also use in reports and presentations.
- Reports: preliminary registers and reports in Word and Excel for review; final reports in Word and PDF.
- Ownership and handover: all data and images are Council's property, stored in Australia during the work, handed over in full at each milestone, and returned or deleted at completion unless Council consents in writing.
