## 1. Project understanding

Council's current framework is the Mid-Western Regional Contributions Plan 2019, prepared for Council by GLN Planning. It applies across the local government area and combines:

- Section 7.11 contributions for residential development, with separate rates for the Mudgee catchment and for the area outside Mudgee (Gulgong, Kandos, Rylstone and the villages);
- Section 7.12 fixed levies on other development; and
- a heavy haulage contribution for extractive industries, based on road pavement damage.

The 2019 works schedule of about $53.3 million covers transport (sealed rural and urban roads, rural bridges, pedestrian access and mobility), open space and recreation, community facilities, Mudgee stormwater and drainage, and plan administration, against forecast growth of about 2,000 residents to 2029.

Council now needs the 2019 Plan, and any later amendments, reviewed and replaced by new Section 7.11 and Section 7.12 plans that reflect current growth and land release, current infrastructure priorities and costs, delivery to date, and current legislation and Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure guidance. Council also wants a framework it can use to prepare and maintain future plans.

A defensible plan rests on three things: a clear nexus between forecast development and each works item, a works schedule that is properly scoped, located and costed, and a reasonable apportionment of each item's cost to new development. JCE's engineering input goes mainly to the second, and supports the planning partner on the first and third.

### Key risks and how JCE will manage them

| Risk | JCE response |
|---|---|
| Out-of-date or inconsistent works costs weaken the plan if challenged | One costing basis for all engineering items (unit rates, on-costs, design and contingency allowances), documented in the background report and verified by the Director before issue. |
| Growth forecasts change during the project | Growth scenario fixed at the end of Stage 2 as a hold point, agreed with Council, before the works schedule is costed. |
| Heavy haulage rate is out of date or hard to defend | Rate rebuilt from current haulage volumes, haul routes, ESA loading and pavement rehabilitation costs, with the calculation set out in a separate memo. |
| Contribution rates exceed the threshold for IPART review | Planning partner checks rates against the threshold at the draft stage, so Council can decide on scope or apportionment before exhibition. |
| Council officer time is stretched | One consolidated comments register, defined review points, and drafts issued complete and checked. |

## 2. Scope of services and methodology

JCE will deliver the services in six stages, with project management throughout. The lead for each task is shown. Hold points (HP) are where Council confirms the output before the next stage starts.

### Project management (all stages)

- Jayden Callen manages the team to one program, with monthly progress reports, one comments and actions register, and design reviews before anything reaches Council.
- JCE coordinates the planning partner and quantity surveyor and is accountable to Council for all deliverables.

### Stage 1: Inception, review and site inspection

- Inception meeting with Council's project team to confirm objectives, scope, data, program, governance and communication.
- Review of the 2019 Plan: works delivered and outstanding, contributions received and held, indexation, and items to carry forward, re-scope or remove (JCE for engineering items; planning partner for plan structure, nexus and administration).
- Review of Council's Delivery Program and Operational Plan, asset management plans, transport, recreation and open space, drainage and flood studies, land use strategies and urban release areas.
- Site inspection of key works locations in Mudgee, Gulgong, Kandos and Rylstone with Council officers (one two-day visit).

### Stage 2: Growth, infrastructure needs and heavy haulage review

- Planning partner: population, dwelling and non-residential growth forecasts for the plan period, by catchment, and occupancy rates.
- JCE: needs assessment for roads, intersections, rural bridges, pathways and pedestrian access, and stormwater and trunk drainage generated by forecast growth, using Council's studies and network data.
- JCE: review of the heavy haulage contribution, including haul routes, current extractive industry volumes, ESA loading, pavement design life and rehabilitation costs, and the per-tonne rate and its indexation.
- JCE and planning partner: nexus and apportionment inputs for each engineering item.
- HP1: Council agrees the growth scenario and the list of works items to be costed.

### Stage 3: Works schedule and cost estimates

- JCE: scoping of each transport and stormwater item (location, extent, standard and staging) and strategic opinions of probable cost on a consistent basis.
- Quantity surveyor: estimates for community, recreation and open space facilities, checked for consistency by JCE.
- Works location maps by catchment and category, and a works schedule spreadsheet with costs, apportionment and timing.
- Director's verification of the works schedule and cost estimates before issue.
- HP2: Council reviews and confirms the works schedule and costs.

### Stage 4: Draft plans and background report

- Planning partner: draft Section 7.11 and Section 7.12 plans, contribution rates, administration provisions, indexation, works-in-kind and planning agreement provisions, and the IPART threshold check.
- JCE: infrastructure background report (transport, stormwater and heavy haulage) and the engineering content of the plans.
- One round of consolidated Council comment, then issue of the drafts for Council's resolution to exhibit (HP3).

### Stage 5: Public exhibition, finalisation and adoption

- Support during public exhibition, responses to submissions (engineering submissions by JCE), amendments to the plans, works schedule and maps, input to the Council report and one Councillor briefing, and handover of final plans and editable files.

### Stage 6: Framework for future contributions plans

- A practical framework and guideline for preparing, reviewing and maintaining contributions plans: data sources, growth review triggers, works scoping and costing method, apportionment, indexation, reporting and the review cycle, with templates for the works schedule and cost model.

The fee includes one round of consolidated Council comment at each draft stage and the meetings listed in Section 5. Further rounds, additional meetings or a second exhibition are additional services under Section 9.

## 4. Exclusions

The following are excluded and can be quoted on request:

- Detailed survey, geotechnical investigation, traffic counts or traffic modelling, and flood or drainage modelling.
- Concept or detailed design of any works item beyond the scoping needed for strategic cost estimates.
- Planning agreements, works-in-kind negotiations, and Section 64 development servicing plans for water and sewer.
- Legal review of the plans, and representation at any IPART review, Land and Environment Court or other proceedings.
- More than one public exhibition, or a second exhibition after material changes by Council.
- Valuation of land for acquisition (a land valuer can be engaged as an additional service if land costs are included).
