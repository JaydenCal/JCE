What we produce

Three outputs, scoped around your site and brief.

01

Concept layout

Proposed lots, an access point, road, basin and easements drawn over your site, so you can test lot mix and yield early.

02

Staging plan

The lots grouped into stages, with the road and services extended in a sensible order, ready to discuss with partners.

03

3D site model

The concept placed on the terrain so lots, access and surroundings can be seen together. Useful for early marketing and funding conversations.

Who it is for

Developers, builders and project owners in regional NSW at the early stage of a subdivision.

Landowners testing a site

Want to see how many lots, what access and which stages might work before spending on detailed design.

Developers briefing partners

Need a clear picture to explain the concept to co-investors, agents and funders at preliminary discussions.

Builders and project owners

Planning a staged release and need a shared view of lots, access and sequence with the project team.

How it works

  1. Share site and brief

    Send the location, any survey or site plans, and what you are considering.

  2. Concept layout

    We draft lots, access and drainage ideas and review them with you.

  3. 3D model and staging

    The layout is built into a 3D site model, with the stages set out.

  4. Present to partners and funders

    Use the plan and model alongside your own project documents.

What this is and isn't

Early, concept level work. Later stages need other people and other documents.

Before we begin

Who provides the feasibility support?

Glide Inside’s director is a licensed civil engineer and can assist with preliminary subdivision feasibility. The work and the outputs are confirmed around your site and brief.

What should I send first?

The property location, your proposed use or lot concept, and any survey, site plans or existing models you have. Add the questions you want to explore and your project timing.

What stage is this work for?

Preliminary feasibility, concept development and project communication. We discuss how you intend to use the work and what further information or specialist input the project needs as it develops.

Can I lodge this with council?

No. The concept is not a survey plan, a development application or engineering design. It helps you organise the early questions and explain the idea. A registered surveyor and your council remain the source for boundaries, levels and planning requirements.

Concepts are for discussion only. They are not survey or development application documentation and do not replace engineering design, planning advice or legal advice. Verify boundaries, levels, services and requirements with a registered surveyor and your local council before relying on any figure shown.
