What a plan can tell you

  • Roughly how a house, shed or set of units sits on the recorded lot.
  • An indicative setback envelope, so you can talk about siting with your builder, designer or council.
  • Areas, counts and layouts that you can take into a conversation.

What it cannot tell you

  • Where the boundary is on the ground. For that you need a registered surveyor.
  • Whether a design is allowed. Planning controls, bushfire, flood, heritage, easements and other overlays have not been checked.
  • Whether a design meets the National Construction Code or any Australian Standard.
  • What a property is worth, or what a project will cost. Figures are estimates, not a valuation or financial advice.

Our position in the terms

Every plan set is a concept for discussion. It is not a regulated design, a survey, an engineering design, a certification or a valuation, and no engineer, surveyor, certifier or council has checked it. The data can be wrong, incomplete or out of date. Check everything with the council, a registered surveyor and suitably qualified people before you rely on it.

This is the position set out in the Plan set terms of use (version 1.1, effective 15/11/2026), which you accept before a preview, PDF or DXF plan set is generated, and the Order terms.
