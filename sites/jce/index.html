Stormwater Engineer

JCE Infrastructure provides stormwater engineering for residential, commercial and subdivision developments of all sizes across regional NSW. That covers stormwater management plans for development applications, complying development and construction certificates, on site detention (OSD) design to the relevant council policy, and pit and pipe drainage design modelled in DRAINS. Where council sets water quality targets, treatment measures are designed and modelled in MUSIC. Working as a drainage engineer to each council's DCP and engineering specification, JCE Infrastructure prepares plans that are ready for assessment, with the supporting calculations the certifier needs. Straightforward house stormwater plans are typically completed within 3 to 5 business days of receiving complete architectural plans and survey.

  • DRAINS
  • MUSIC
  • Council DCP and OSD policy

Flood Engineer and Flood Impact Assessment

JCE Infrastructure provides flood engineering for development on flood prone land. A flood impact assessment establishes how a site behaves in the 1% AEP flood and rarer events, confirms the flood planning level and the floor levels council will accept, and demonstrates that the development will not worsen flooding on neighbouring land. Flood modelling follows Australian Rainfall and Runoff 2019 (ARR 2019) and the NSW Flood Risk Management Manual, using council flood studies and mapping where they are available. As a flood consultant, JCE Infrastructure supports development applications across regional NSW, including in the floodplain towns of Moree, Gunnedah, Grafton and Port Macquarie, and gives flood immunity advice for roads and infrastructure.

  • ARR 2019
  • NSW Flood Risk Management Manual
  • 1% AEP and freeboard

Hydraulic Engineer

JCE Infrastructure provides hydraulic engineering for land development and civil infrastructure. Hydraulic design covers stormwater drainage networks, culverts and channels, water supply and sewerage systems, pump stations and rising mains, including surge assessment for pressurised pipelines. Hydraulic plans are prepared for development applications and for construction, coordinated with the architectural and civil design. Where a project also needs building hydraulic or fire services design, JCE Infrastructure engages specialist hydraulic engineers and coordinates their input within a single scope, so you have one hydraulic consultant. The principal engineer, who certifies civil and hydraulic designs as a suitably qualified person for NSW work, deals with you directly. Clients include developers, builders, councils and water authorities across regional NSW.

Water and Sewer Design

JCE Infrastructure designs water and sewer infrastructure for subdivisions, commercial developments and council works, to the WSAA Water Supply Code and Sewerage Code and the requirements of the relevant water authority. The work includes water reticulation and main extensions, gravity sewer design, pressure sewer systems designed to WSA 07, sewage pump station design and rising mains, with surge analysis and septicity assessment. Designs follow the authority's own specifications and standard drawings so they can go to approval and construction. Recent work includes a sewer rising main for the Western Sydney Airport business precinct. Water and sewer design is provided for developers and councils across regional NSW and the wider state.

Swept Path Analysis

Councils commonly require a swept path analysis, also called a turning path analysis, to show that the design vehicle can enter, manoeuvre and leave a site without conflict. JCE Infrastructure prepares swept path drawings for driveways, car parks, loading docks and waste collection points, using design vehicles from passenger cars to heavy rigid trucks and B-doubles, in accordance with AS 2890 and Austroads. The analysis is combined with driveway design, including grades and vehicle crossings to the council's standard drawings, so access issues are resolved before the development application is lodged. Swept path analysis is provided for residential, commercial and industrial sites across regional NSW.

  • AS 2890
  • Austroads design vehicles
  • Council standard drawings

Erosion and Sediment Control Plans

Most councils require an erosion and sediment control plan (ESCP) with a development application, and the controls must be installed before earthworks begin. JCE Infrastructure prepares ESCPs for house sites, commercial developments and subdivisions in accordance with Managing Urban Stormwater: Soils and Construction, known as the Blue Book, and the council's DCP. Plans show sediment fencing, stabilised site access, diversion drains, stockpile locations and, where the disturbed area warrants it, sediment basins sized by the Blue Book method. Larger sites receive a soil and water management plan. ESCPs are prepared across regional NSW, on their own or as part of a stormwater or civil design package.

  • Blue Book (Landcom)
  • Council DCP
  • Soil and water management plans

Pavement Design

JCE Infrastructure provides pavement design for roads, car parks, hardstands and heavy vehicle areas. Flexible and rigid pavements are designed in accordance with the Austroads Guide to Pavement Technology Part 2 (AGPT02), and industrial slabs are designed for the forklift, truck and storage loads they will carry. Each design is based on the design traffic and the subgrade strength from the geotechnical investigation, and follows the council's engineering specification where the pavement will become a public asset. Pavement design is provided for subdivisions, commercial and industrial sites, rural roads and haul roads across regional NSW, and is shown layer by layer in the interactive subdivision model.

  • Austroads AGPT02
  • Flexible and rigid pavements

Site Classification

A site classification to AS 2870 describes how much the soil on a block is expected to move, and it sets the basis for the slab and footing design of a new dwelling, extension or granny flat. JCE Infrastructure arranges the geotechnical investigation, prepares the site classification report and completes the footing design, so the classification and the slab design come from one engineer. Reactive clay soils across the New England North West and other parts of regional NSW often call for stiffened raft slabs or deeper footings, and each design responds to the classification and to the cut and fill on the site. Footing design is completed with our structural engineer where required.

  • AS 2870
  • Site classification report
  • Footing design

Common questions

Before you ask for a quote

Which areas do you cover?

JCE Infrastructure works across NSW, on metropolitan, regional and rural projects. Site visits, travel and local requirements are scoped in your fee proposal.

Do I need a civil engineer for my development application?

Councils generally want civil engineering input when a development involves a new driveway or car park, changes to stormwater, earthworks or retaining walls, flood affected land, or new water and sewer connections. If you are unsure, send the site address and plans and we will tell you what the council is likely to require.

What is in a stormwater management plan?

Usually a catchment plan, the pit and pipe layout, on-site detention sizing where the council requires it, water quality measures, and erosion and sediment control, with the supporting calculations.

Do I need on-site detention?

It depends on the council and the site. Many councils require on-site detention when a development adds roof and paved area, so the site sends no more stormwater downstream than it did before. We check the policy for your address and size the storage where it is required.

When does council require a flood impact assessment?

Generally when a development is on flood prone land or inside the flood planning area on the council's mapping. The assessment shows how the site behaves in the 1% AEP flood, confirms that floor levels meet the flood planning level (commonly the 1% AEP flood level plus freeboard), and shows that the development will not worsen flooding on other land.

What is a swept path analysis?

A drawing that tracks a design vehicle through the site to show it can enter, turn and leave without hitting kerbs, walls or parked vehicles. Councils and Transport for NSW ask for one for driveways, car parks, loading areas and waste collection points.

What do you need from me to quote?

The site address, the plans or a sketch, and any correspondence or consent conditions from the council. A survey and a geotechnical report also help if you have them.

Do I need an engineer for a granny flat or an extension?

In most cases, yes. Councils and private certifiers generally require a stormwater plan, and many also require an engineered slab or footing design. A new driveway crossing or a retaining wall has its own requirements. Send the site address and plans and we will confirm what applies.

Do you work with private certifiers and complying development?

Yes. We scope civil documentation for DA, CDC, CC and subdivision works applications. Specialist certification and regulated designs are identified separately, and the practitioners and requirements are confirmed before engagement.

Can you respond to a council request for information?

Yes. We review the council's request, prepare the civil and stormwater response, and liaise with the council's engineer where needed.

How long does a stormwater plan take?

Straightforward house stormwater plans typically take 3 to 5 business days after we receive complete architectural plans and survey. More complex sites take longer, and the programme is confirmed in the fee proposal.

What does it cost?

Fees depend on the site and the council's requirements. You receive a written fee proposal with the scope and fee before any work starts.
