Stormwater Engineer

JCE Infrastructure works as a stormwater engineer on houses, commercial sites and subdivisions across regional NSW. Most councils ask for a stormwater management plan with the development application, complying development certificate or construction certificate. We prepare it to the council's DCP and engineering specification, with the calculations your certifier needs. Where council policy calls for on site detention, the OSD design sizes the tank or basin and its outlet so the site releases no more water than it did before. Pit and pipe networks are modelled in DRAINS, and where council sets water quality targets, treatment is checked with MUSIC modelling. If you need a drainage engineer to answer a council request for information, send the letter with your plans. Straightforward house stormwater plans are typically finished in 3 to 5 business days once we have complete architectural plans and survey.

  • DRAINS
  • MUSIC
  • Council DCP and OSD policy

Flood Engineer and Flood Impact Assessment

Building or subdividing on flood prone land usually means council wants a flood impact assessment before it will determine the application. As your flood engineer, JCE Infrastructure works out how the site behaves in the 1% AEP flood and rarer events, confirms the flood planning level and the floor levels council will accept, and shows that the development will not make flooding worse on neighbouring land. Flood modelling follows Australian Rainfall and Runoff 2019 (ARR 2019) and the NSW Flood Risk Management Manual, starting from council's flood study and mapping where they exist. We also act as flood consultant on roads and infrastructure that need a flood immunity check. Floodplain sites across the New England North West and the Mid North Coast make up a regular part of the work.

  • ARR 2019
  • NSW Flood Risk Management Manual
  • 1% AEP and freeboard

Hydraulic Engineer

JCE Infrastructure provides civil hydraulic design for land development and infrastructure. The work covers stormwater networks, culverts and open channels, water supply and sewerage systems, pump stations and rising mains, with surge checks on pressurised pipelines. Hydraulic plans are prepared for the development application and for construction, and coordinated with the architectural and civil drawings. Jayden certifies civil and hydraulic designs as a suitably qualified person for NSW work. When a building also needs hydraulic or fire services design, we bring in specialist hydraulic engineers and coordinate their input under one scope, so you have a single hydraulic consultant from fee proposal to approval. Developers, builders and councils across regional NSW use the service for subdivisions, commercial sites and council works.

Water and Sewer Design

JCE Infrastructure designs water and sewer infrastructure for subdivisions, commercial developments and council works. Designs follow the WSAA codes, including the Water Supply Code and Sewerage Code, together with the water authority's own supplements, specifications and standard drawings, so they can move on to approval and construction. The work covers water reticulation and main extensions, gravity sewer design, pressure sewer systems designed to WSA 07, sewage pump station design and rising mains, with surge and septicity checks where a main is long or undulating. Recent work includes the sewer rising main for the Western Sydney Airport business precinct. Across most of regional NSW the council is also the water authority, and we design to its requirements from the first concept.

  • WSAA codes
  • WSA 07 pressure sewer
  • Authority supplements

Swept Path Analysis

Councils often ask for a swept path analysis, also called a turning path analysis, to show that the design vehicle can enter, turn and leave a site without striking kerbs, walls or parked cars. JCE Infrastructure prepares swept path drawings for driveways, car parks, loading docks and waste collection points, using design vehicles from a passenger car up to heavy rigid trucks and B-doubles, in line with AS 2890 and Austroads. We usually pair it with driveway design: grades, transitions and the vehicle crossing to the council's standard drawings, so the access works in section as well as in plan. Sorting out access before lodgement saves a council request for information later. The service covers residential, commercial and industrial sites across regional NSW.

  • AS 2890
  • Austroads design vehicles
  • Council standard drawings

Erosion and Sediment Control Plans

Most councils want an erosion and sediment control plan (ESCP) with the DA, and the controls must be in place before earthworks start. JCE Infrastructure prepares ESCPs for house sites, commercial developments and subdivisions to Managing Urban Stormwater: Soils and Construction, the Landcom guideline known as the Blue Book, and to the council's DCP. The plan shows sediment fencing, the stabilised site access, diversion drains and stockpile locations. Where the disturbed area warrants one, sediment basins are sized by the Blue Book method, and larger sites get a full soil and water management plan. An ESCP for a DA can be prepared on its own or as part of a stormwater or civil design package, anywhere in regional NSW.

  • Blue Book (Landcom)
  • Council DCP
  • Soil and water management plans

Pavement Design

JCE Infrastructure designs pavements for subdivision roads, car parks, hardstands and heavy vehicle areas. Flexible and rigid pavements are designed to the Austroads Guide to Pavement Technology Part 2 (AGPT02), using the design traffic and the subgrade strength from the geotechnical investigation. Industrial slabs are designed for the forklift, truck and storage loads they will carry. Where a road will become a council asset, the design follows that council's engineering specification. The service covers subdivisions, commercial and industrial sites, rural roads and haul roads across regional NSW. The 3D subdivision model on this page shows a pavement build-up layer by layer, next to the cut and fill that sets the road and lot levels.

Site Classification

A site classification to AS 2870 rates how much the soil on a block is expected to shrink and swell, and it sets the basis for the slab and footing design of a new house, extension or granny flat. JCE Infrastructure arranges the geotechnical investigation, prepares the site classification report and completes the footing design, so the classification and the slab come from one engineer. The reactive clays found across the New England North West and other parts of regional NSW often call for stiffened raft slabs or deeper footings, and each footing design responds to the site class and to the cut and fill on the block. Footing design is completed with our structural engineer where required. Send the address and house plans to get a fee proposal for the site classification report and footing design together.

  • AS 2870
  • Site classification report
  • Footing design

Common questions

Before you ask for a quote

When does council need a stormwater management plan?

Most NSW councils ask for a stormwater management plan with a development application, complying development certificate or construction certificate whenever a project adds roof or paved area, changes how water leaves the site, or needs on-site detention. The plan shows the catchment, the pit and pipe layout, any detention and water quality measures, and the point where water leaves the site, with supporting calculations. If you are unsure, send the address and plans and JCE Infrastructure will tell you what your council is likely to require.

When does council require a flood impact assessment?

Generally when the land is flood prone or sits inside the flood planning area on council's mapping. The assessment shows how the site behaves in the 1% AEP flood, confirms that floor levels meet the flood planning level (commonly the 1% AEP flood level plus freeboard), and shows that the development will not make flooding worse on other land.

Do I need on-site detention?

It depends on the council and the site. Many councils require on-site detention when a development adds roof and paved area, so the site releases no more stormwater than it did before. JCE Infrastructure checks the policy that applies to your address and sizes the storage where it is needed.

How long does a stormwater plan take?

Straightforward house stormwater plans typically take 3 to 5 business days after we receive complete architectural plans and survey. Larger or more complex sites take longer, and the programme is confirmed in the fee proposal.

What do you need from me to quote?

The site address, the plans or a sketch, and any council correspondence or consent conditions. A survey and a geotechnical report help where you have them. Fee proposals are issued within 1 to 2 business days.

Which standards and software do you design to?

Water and sewer work follows the WSAA codes (including WSA 07 for pressure sewer) and the water authority's own supplements. Stormwater is modelled in DRAINS and water quality in MUSIC, to the council's guidelines. Flood work follows Australian Rainfall and Runoff 2019 and the NSW Flood Risk Management Manual. Roads and pavements follow Austroads, car parks and access follow AS 2890, and site classification follows AS 2870.
